What the sample is made of

Random audits are the only representative read of quality. CSAT, targeted and complaint audits are opened because something already went wrong, so their scores measure the investigation, not the operation. Averaging them together produces a number that is neither.

What this says

Two different percentages appear below and they answer different questions. Share of vertical's exceptions tells you which call reason dominates that vertical's problems. Rate tells you how often the problem occurs across all audits of that vertical — that is the one to use when comparing Airport against Hourly.

The "Others" problem

Risk audits are shown against the agent's full audit count, so a agent with 12 risk audits out of 30 is never confused with one who has 12 out of 12. Sort by risk rate, not risk count.

Repeat quality risk

Agents with two or more Tier C/D audits in the period. A coaching queue for team leads — not an HR ranking.

Recurring same-reason patterns

The same agent, vertical and call reason appearing twice or more in the risk population. This points at a knowledge or process gap rather than a bad day.

All audited agents

Scores are compared across auditors to surface calibration drift. A consistent gap in average score or tier mix between auditors usually means the rubric is being read differently, not that one auditor's agents are worse.
